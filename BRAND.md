# The Redding Supper Club

## The idea: a table with intention

An independent supper-club identity rooted in the North State. The feeling is a handwritten dinner invitation meets a considered editorial journal: warm, grounded, quietly optimistic, and genuinely welcoming.

**Brand line:** Good food. Better questions.

**Supporting promise:** Leave with perspective, not just contacts.

**Positioning:** A curated, hosted dinner for people building, leading, making, and rethinking what comes next in the North State and beyond. Thoughtful curation serves a better conversation, rather than status or exclusivity.

**Typography:** Instrument Serif for expressive headlines and italic emphasis; DM Sans for clear, understated practical information. Both are self-hosted, with their SIL Open Font License notices in `assets/`.

**Emblem:** `assets/emblem.svg` is a custom, single-color circular mark combining a mountain horizon and a shared table. It connects the local landscape to the event’s central idea. Use with the Redding / Supper Club wordmark or by itself as a small seal.

**Photography:** Real table textures, shared food, warm light, and human-scale details. Images establish the mood; the included stock photos do not depict Trilogy Guild or actual attendees.

**Graphic details:** Fine rules, generous margins, an eight-point conversational spark, a printed date card, and a terracotta “Come curious / Leave inspired” seal. A custom plate-and-gear drawing connects the automation topic to dinner without making the brand feel like a tech conference.

**Host brands:** Meet Near Me and SmartLemon remain recognizable partner marks, with the supper club’s own identity leading the page.

## Voice

Inviting, direct, thoughtful. Speak like a good host, not an event promoter. Use “table,” “conversation,” “curiosity,” and “perspective.” Explain how invitations work without suggesting that attendees need prestige, credentials, or permission to belong.

- “Redding has interesting people you haven’t met yet.”
- “Different worlds. One shared table.”
- “No keynote. No pitches. No networking circuit.”
- “You don’t need a title. Just something you care about.”
